India has two personal tax regimes. The new regime is the default; individuals can choose the old regime when filing (salaried people can switch each year; those with business income have restrictions).

## New regime
Lower slab rates and a standard deduction for salaried people (₹75,000), but most deductions are not allowed, including the tax-saving investment deduction (old 80C), HRA and home-loan interest on a self-occupied house. After Budget 2025, a rebate means no tax is payable on taxable income up to ₹12 lakh (about ₹12.75 lakh for salaried people after the standard deduction). The rebate generally does not cover special-rate income such as capital gains on equity.

## Old regime
Higher slab rates but allows deductions: tax-saving investments up to ₹1.5 lakh, an extra ₹50,000 for NPS, health insurance premiums, HRA, home-loan interest and others.

## Which is better?
It depends on how many deductions you actually claim. People with large deductions (home loan, HRA, full tax-saving investments) may pay less under the old regime; many others pay less under the new one. Compare both with the official calculator before choosing. Note: the Income-tax Act, 2025 renumbered the sections, so the familiar "80C" now has a new section number.

Rules as of: Finance Act 2026 (tax year 2026-27, Income-tax Act, 2025). Tax rules change; confirm on incometax.gov.in or with a tax professional before filing.
