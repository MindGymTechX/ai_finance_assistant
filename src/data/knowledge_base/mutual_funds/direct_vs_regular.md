Every mutual fund scheme has two plans with the same portfolio and fund manager:
- Regular plan: bought through a distributor; the distributor's commission is paid from the scheme's expenses.
- Direct plan: bought directly from the fund house or through direct-plan platforms; no distributor commission.

## The difference
Direct plans have a lower expense ratio, often by around 0.5-1.5% a year for equity funds, so their NAV grows faster. Over 15-20 years that gap compounds into a large amount.

## Trade-off
A regular plan may include guidance from the distributor. Investors who want advice can also pay a SEBI-registered investment adviser a fee and invest in direct plans. Switching from regular to direct is a redemption and fresh purchase, which can trigger capital gains tax and exit loads.
