For learning only, not a recommendation.
1. Category fit: does the fund's category match the goal's horizon and risk?
2. Consistency: rolling returns over 3, 5 and 7 years versus its benchmark and peers, not just last year's return.
3. Risk: how much it fell in bad markets; standard deviation and downside capture.
4. Cost: expense ratio, especially direct vs regular plan.
5. Portfolio: concentration in top holdings and sectors; overlap with funds you already own.
6. Fund house and manager: process, tenure and governance.
7. Size: very small funds can be volatile; very large small-cap funds can struggle to deploy money.
Owning many funds of the same category usually adds overlap, not diversification.
