An Initial Public Offering (IPO) is when a company first sells shares to the public and lists on an exchange.

- Applications are made through ASBA (money is blocked, not debited, until allotment) or UPI.
- Shares are allotted by lottery when an issue is oversubscribed; retail investors may get nothing.
- Read the offer document (DRHP/RHP) for business risks, use of money raised and valuation.
- Listing-day gains are not guaranteed; some popular IPOs have listed below the issue price.
- Applying only because of "grey market premium" chatter is speculation, not investing.
