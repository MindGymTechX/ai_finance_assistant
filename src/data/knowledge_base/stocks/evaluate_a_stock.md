This checklist is for learning how analysts think; it is not a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

1. Understand the business: what it sells, who its customers are, and its competitive advantage.
2. Growth: revenue and profit trend over 5-10 years, not one quarter.
3. Profitability: return on equity (ROE) and operating margins compared with peers.
4. Balance sheet: debt-to-equity, interest coverage; heavy debt raises risk.
5. Cash flow: profits should turn into operating cash.
6. Management and governance: promoter holding, pledged shares, related-party deals, auditor remarks.
7. Valuation: P/E, price-to-book and others versus peers and history.
8. Risks: regulation, competition, concentration of customers or products.
9. Fit: how the stock would change your own diversification and risk.

Individual stock picking takes time and skill; diversified funds let beginners own many companies without this work.
