Compound interest means you earn returns not only on the money you invested but also on the returns already earned. Over time this "interest on interest" makes money grow faster than simple interest.

## Example
₹1,00,000 growing at 10% a year becomes about ₹1,10,000 after 1 year, ₹1,61,000 after 5 years, ₹2,59,000 after 10 years and ₹6,73,000 after 20 years. The last 10 years add far more than the first 10, because the base keeps growing.

## Why it matters
- Time is the biggest driver: starting 5 years earlier often matters more than investing a little more each month.
- Costs and taxes compound too. A fund with a 1.5% higher yearly expense ratio can take a large bite out of 20-year returns.
- The rule of 72 is a quick estimate: divide 72 by the yearly return to get the years needed to double. At 8% money doubles in about 9 years; at 12% in about 6.
