Because of compounding, money invested early has more years to grow.

## Example (illustrative 10% yearly return)
- Asha invests ₹5,000 a month from age 25 to 35 (10 years), then stops but stays invested until 60.
- Ravi invests ₹5,000 a month from 35 to 60 (25 years).
Asha invests ₹6 lakh in total and Ravi ₹15 lakh, yet Asha ends with roughly ₹1.1 crore versus Ravi's roughly ₹62 lakh, because her money compounded for longer.

## Lessons
- Small amounts started early beat larger amounts started late.
- Consistency matters more than timing the market.
- Returns are never guaranteed; the example only shows how time multiplies growth.
