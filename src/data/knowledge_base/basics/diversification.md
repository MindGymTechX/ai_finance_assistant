Diversification means spreading money across different investments so that one bad outcome does not damage the whole portfolio.

## Levels of diversification
- Across companies: holding many stocks instead of a few.
- Across sectors: IT, banking, consumer, healthcare and others react differently to events.
- Across asset classes: equity, debt, gold and cash behave differently.
- Across time: investing regularly (SIP) instead of all at once.

## Concentration risk
If 60% of a portfolio is in IT stocks, a slowdown in IT spending hits most of the money at once. Many educational guides suggest no single stock above about 10-20% and no single sector above about 30-40% of a portfolio, but these are rules of thumb, not laws.

## Easy diversification
A single index fund or diversified mutual fund already holds dozens of companies across sectors, which is why beginners often start there.
