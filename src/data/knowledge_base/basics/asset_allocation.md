Asset allocation is how your money is divided among asset classes such as equity, debt, gold and cash. Research has long found that allocation explains most of the variation in a portfolio's returns, more than the choice of individual funds.

## How it is usually decided
- Time horizon: longer goals can hold more equity.
- Risk appetite: how much decline you can tolerate without panic-selling.
- Goal importance: essential goals (a child's education) often use a more cautious mix than optional ones.

## Illustrative mixes (education, not a recommendation)
- Cautious: around 20-40% equity, rest in debt and some gold.
- Moderate: around 50-70% equity.
- Aggressive: around 70-90% equity.
A well-known shortcut is "100 minus age" in equity; it is crude and ignores goals and risk appetite.

## Rebalancing
Markets move the mix over time. Rebalancing (once a year, or when the mix drifts by more than 5-10 points) brings it back to the target and enforces "sell high, buy low" discipline.
